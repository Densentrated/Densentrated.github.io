ECG Amplifier

Problem

The class assignment was to amplify the electrical signal from the heart in order to observe its rhythm, while minimizing noise. The processed analog signal would then be read by an ADC and passed into MATLAB for additional digital filtering.

Solution

The core challenge was boosting the heart's signal while rejecting common-mode noise — the kind of interference that appears equally on both measurement leads. An instrumentation amplifier was the right tool for this: it provides high differential gain while effectively eliminating common-mode gain. From there, the signal passed through a high-pass filter to strip out the DC component, and then a low-pass filter to cut high-frequency noise. Together, these three stages formed the complete analog signal chain.

After designing the circuit, we built it on a breadboard and tested the output at each stage — raw signal, post-instrumentation amplifier, post-high-pass, and post-low-pass — to verify each stage was performing correctly before moving on. The conditioned signal was then read into MATLAB via an ADC, where we applied additional digital filters to further clean up the waveform and make the heart's rhythm clearly visible.

Results

Using the processed signal, we were able to calculate the user's BPM from the waveform. The project was also a solid hands-on introduction to both analog and digital filter design and how the two approaches complement each other in a real signal processing pipeline.
